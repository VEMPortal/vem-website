What We Offer — Strategy Platform

A Model Lineup Built for Portfolio Management

One disciplined process feeds every strategy we offer. The platform spans active equity SMAs, rules-based ETF models, selectively used structured notes, and fully customized client portfolios — all governed by the same quantitative-plus-fundamental approach and fiduciary oversight.

Stock-by-stock SMA portfolios — US Large Cap Growth, US Large Cap Value, and International ADR — built through the firm's quantitative-plus-fundamental process, typically 40–65 holdings per strategy, benchmarked to the Russell 1000 Growth, Russell 1000 Value, and MSCI EAFE respectively.

Cost-efficient exchange-traded funds scored on efficiency, tradability, fit, raw and risk-adjusted performance, and downside/tail risk — additional models added only when approved and sourced.

Income and growth notes used only where the risk/reward profile fits the client mandate — each note reviewed for issuer credit, reference assets, barriers and buffers, call features, and liquidity. Not suitable for all investors.

Every engagement narrows to one outcome: a portfolio shaped to the client's risk tolerance, tax considerations, and preferences — built on the same disciplined process that drives the firm's model strategies.

Structured Notes

Used Selectively, Reviewed Rigorously

Income Notes

Structured investments designed to provide periodic income potential, typically linked to the performance of one or more underlying reference assets.

  • Issuer and credit exposure
  • Underlying reference assets
  • Coupon structure and payment conditions
  • Call features and no-call periods
  • Barriers, buffers, and downside exposure
  • Liquidity and suitability considerations

Structured notes involve risks, including issuer credit risk, market risk, liquidity risk, call risk, complexity risk, and possible loss of principal. They are not suitable for all investors.

Growth Notes

Structured investments designed to provide market-participation potential, often linked to one or more indices, securities, or baskets.

  • Upside participation structure
  • Downside exposure and barrier/buffer terms
  • Issuer and credit exposure
  • Tenor and call schedule
  • Reference asset composition
  • Fit within the broader portfolio

Structured notes involve risks, including issuer credit risk, market risk, liquidity risk, call risk, complexity risk, and possible loss of principal. They are not suitable for all investors.
