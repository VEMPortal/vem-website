Staying Anchored When Markets Get Loud

Vann Equity Management June 9, 2026 2 min read

Periods of market volatility share a common feature: they feel, in the moment, as though they will never end. The headlines grow louder, the forecasts grow more certain, and the temptation to do something grows with them. Yet the long arc of markets has rewarded those who stayed anchored far more often than those who reacted.

This note is not a forecast. It is a reminder of the framework we return to whenever markets get loud.

Volatility is a feature, not a flaw

Equity returns and equity volatility are inseparable. The same market that has rewarded long-term owners of good businesses also delivers the periodic declines that test their conviction. Trying to capture the former while avoiding the latter is, for most investors, a reliable way to do worse than simply staying invested.

We treat volatility as the cost of a long-term return stream — a cost worth paying, and occasionally an opportunity worth using.

Three anchors

When the environment turns uncertain, we hold to three things:

  1. Own quality. Durable businesses with strong balance sheets and capable management tend to come through difficult periods intact — and often stronger relative to weaker competitors.
  2. Mind the price paid. The price you pay for an asset is the single greatest determinant of your long-term return. Disruption sometimes offers better prices on businesses we already admire.
  3. Keep the time horizon honest. Money that may be needed soon does not belong in equities. Money invested for the long term should be allowed to behave like a long-term investment.

What we are doing

In practice, our response to volatility is usually measured rather than dramatic. We revisit the businesses we own and ask whether the facts have changed or only the prices have. Where the long-term thesis is intact and the price has improved, volatility works in our favor. Where the facts have genuinely deteriorated, we act accordingly.

What we do not do is allow short-term noise to override a process built for the long term.

Markets will continue to surprise us — that is their nature. A steady framework, consistently applied, remains the most dependable response we know.
