Introduction

Vann Equity Management, LLC (“VEM”) is registered with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (“SEC”) as an investment adviser. Investment advisory services and brokerage services are fundamentally different; understanding the differences is important when choosing a financial services firm. This document gives you a summary of the types of advisory services we provide and how you pay for them, and helps you understand your rights when working with us.

Free and simple tools are available to research firms and financial professionals at www.investor.gov/CRS, which also provides educational materials about investment advisers, broker-dealers, and investing.

What Investment Services and Advice Can You Provide Me?

VEM offers investment advisory services to retail investors on a discretionary basis, meaning we make the day-to-day investment decisions for your account without requiring your prior approval for each trade. We manage equity and tactical asset-allocation separately managed accounts (SMAs) for individuals, families, institutions, endowments, and family offices.

Types of Accounts & Services

  • Separately Managed Accounts (SMAs): We manage your account at a third-party custodian (such as Fidelity, Schwab, or another qualified custodian you select). You retain ownership of the individual securities in your account at all times.
  • Equity Portfolios: We manage U.S. equity portfolios applying VEM’s proprietary quantitative and fundamental research process.
  • Tactical Asset Allocation Portfolios: We manage portfolios that may hold equities, fixed income, and cash equivalents depending on our assessment of market conditions.

Investment Monitoring

We continuously monitor your portfolio. We review each client’s account on an ongoing basis and provide periodic performance reports. We will contact you to discuss material changes in your financial situation, investment objectives, or risk tolerance.

Investment Authority

We manage all client accounts on a discretionary basis pursuant to a signed investment management agreement. Our investment authority is limited only by any written restrictions you provide to us at account opening or in writing thereafter.

Account Minimums

VEM generally requires a minimum account size of $250,000. We may waive or reduce this minimum at our sole discretion.

What Fees Will I Pay?

The principal fee for our investment advisory services is an asset-based management fee calculated as a percentage of the assets we manage for you. Fees are generally billed quarterly, in advance, based on the market value of your account at the end of the prior quarter.

Asset-Based Management Fee

Our standard annual fee schedule is as follows:

  • First $1,000,000: 1.00% annually
  • Next $4,000,000: 0.75% annually
  • Assets above $5,000,000: negotiable

Fees may be negotiated. The specific fee applicable to your account will be set out in your investment management agreement.

Other Fees and Costs

In addition to our advisory fee, you will also pay other fees and costs, including:

  • Custodian Fees: Fees charged by the broker-dealer or bank that holds your account. These may include transaction costs, account maintenance fees, and other service charges.
  • Transaction Costs: Costs related to buying and selling securities in your account, such as brokerage commissions.
  • Mutual Fund and ETF Expenses: If your portfolio includes mutual funds or exchange-traded funds, you will also bear the internal management fees and operating expenses of those funds in addition to our advisory fee.

You will pay fees and costs whether you make or lose money on your investments. Fees and costs will reduce any amount of money you make on your investments over time. Please make sure you understand what fees and costs you are paying.

What Are Your Legal Obligations to Me? How Else Does Your Firm Make Money, and What Conflicts of Interest Do You Have?

When we act as your investment adviser, we are held to a fiduciary standard. This means we must act in your best interest and not put our own interests ahead of yours. At the same time, the way we make money creates some conflicts with your interests. You should understand and ask us about these conflicts, because they affect the recommendations and investment advice we provide you.

How Do We Make Money?

VEM is compensated primarily through asset-based management fees paid directly by clients. We do not receive commissions, 12b-1 fees, sales loads, or any other third-party compensation for recommending specific securities or products.

Conflicts of Interest

  • Asset-Based Fees: Because our advisory fees are based on assets under management, we have a financial incentive to encourage you to increase the amount of assets you place under our management.
  • Custodian Selection: We may recommend specific custodians. While we seek to recommend custodians based on best execution and overall service quality, there may be an element of convenience to us in consolidating clients at certain custodians.

For more information about our conflicts of interest, please see our Form ADV Part 2A Brochure (Items 10, 11, and 14). Additional information is also available at www.investor.gov/CRS.

How Do Your Financial Professionals Make Money?

VEM’s financial professionals are compensated primarily through a combination of salary and a share of the firm’s overall profitability, which is linked to the firm’s total assets under management. Our professionals do not receive commissions or sales incentives for recommending particular investments or services.

The primary financial professional responsible for your account is John A. Vann, who is also the Managing Partner and majority owner of VEM. As an owner of the firm, Mr. Vann benefits from growth in the firm’s assets under management. This creates a financial incentive that could influence the advice he provides to you.

Do You or Your Financial Professionals Have Legal or Disciplinary History?

No. Neither VEM nor any of our financial professionals has any reportable legal or disciplinary history. We have not been subject to any legal or disciplinary event that a reasonable retail investor would consider important in deciding whether to work with us or entrust their assets to our management.
